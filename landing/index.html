MC Zone

MC Zone was a Minecraft server network from 2012 to 2013, home to minigames like The Walls, Sky Wars, Hunger Games, Survival Games and Nebula. At its peak, 3,500 people were playing at once.

This archive brings back its old websites from the Wayback Machine, tells its story from the first server in 2011, and remembers the people who made it. The story carries on into what MC Zone became, the Avicus Network.

Put together by Keenan Thompson (funkystudios), who founded MC Zone.

About this archive

Minecraft is a video game where you build things out of blocks, alone or with others. Anyone can run their own Minecraft server, an online world that other players join over the internet, and in 2012 and 2013 those servers were one of the most creative corners of online gaming. The game didn't come with minigames, short competitive matches like last-one-standing battles or capture the flag, so players invented their own. Players, many of them teenagers, wrote the code, built the maps (the worlds each match is played in) and ran whole networks of servers to host them, years before Roblox and Fortnite made games created by players mainstream.

MC Zone was one of those networks. More than a million people played on it, many of them for hours a day every summer, and for a lot of them it was a central part of growing up. People built identities around their usernames, and found an outlet for their creativity in building maps, inventing game modes and writing code, while volunteer moderators kept the peace and helped run a community of thousands.

Almost all of it has disappeared since. The servers are long gone, the website went offline years ago, and what's left is scattered across the Wayback Machine, old YouTube videos and people's memories. This archive puts it back together in one place so it isn't lost. I also hope it helps people from back then find each other again, whether to share a story, swap memories or just say hello. If you were there, get in touch.

Timeline

  1. 2011

  2. AugustAt 14, funkystudios opens TerraScape, a Minecraft server for friends, run at first from a computer at home. It starts out as a creative server with "a grand total of 5 dedicated users", then opens to the public as a survival server where players fight monsters rather than each other, and pays its way through players buying higher ranks. Listed on server sites, it grows to as many as 40 players online at once.
    SourcesAbout page (2012)About page (2013)
  3. OctoberTerraScape reinvents itself as a city server. Players take jobs to earn money, buy lots inside cities and arrive at a brand-new spawn, which the website describes as "TSS TerraScape Station. A massive train station where you can take a train to a city, or head out and build in the wild." Factions follow in January 2012, along with VIP, VIP+ and VIP++ donor ranks. Donors keep it running: in 2011 that includes funkystudios' middle school friends, and in January 2012 my0445316 starts paying for TerraScape's server.
    SourcesTerraScape newsPlugins

  4. 2012

  5. Earlyfunkystudios starts a Hunger Games server. Everyone spawns in a freshly generated world with a small kit, and up to 40 players fight until one is left. When the Hunger Games film arrives in March 2012, players go looking for the real thing in Minecraft. There is no official version, so server owners build it themselves as Bukkit plugins, add-ons that change how a Minecraft server plays, and Hunger Games servers spring up everywhere, MCPVP among the most popular. funkystudios' server, mc-hg.org, starts out on a free plugin by ftbastler, and funkystudios learns to program by decompiling plugins like it and tweaking them, later writing custom ones from scratch. A community grows around it, and it expands to four or five servers, each with its own address like b.mc-hg.org. Game stats are tracked within a week of the move to four servers.
    SourcesPlanet Minecraft listing
  6. SummerIn June, Hypixel, then a map maker on YouTube, releases The Walls as an adventure map. funkystudios writes a plugin to run it as a multiplayer server game, later described as "the first server to host the walls on an automatic basis without interruptions". Still a separate server from Hunger Games, it grows to three servers and becomes funkystudios' most popular game yet.
    SourcesAbout page (2013)
  7. AugustWith funkystudios busy with other projects, TerraScape dwindles to no players over the summer and shuts down. my0445316 later brings it back.SourcesAbout page (2013)and funkystudios' recollection
  8. NovemberEach new server has its moment before players drift on to the next game, so funkystudios ties them all together. As the 2012 About page puts it: "I realized what I was missing - a link between all of the gaming servers I have developed, so I made MC Zone." MC Zone launches as one site for every game, and TerraScape is handed over to my0445316, who takes on its server costs and later becomes one of MC Zone's administrators. MC Zone is one of the early servers to track detailed player stats: kills, deaths, wins, losses, games played and time online, with a leaderboard for every game, and later profiles showing each player's recent matches, PvP kills and forum activity. Kit PVP and SkyBlock round out the list. By MC Zone's own account, it reaches 500 players online within two months and 1,000 within two and a half.SourcesAbout page (2012)May 2013 staffv1v2
  9. November 30MC Zone passes 300 players online for the first time.
  10. Winter, into 2013Sky Wars arrives: two teams battle it out on floating islands. The concept is JJtCool's, and MC Zone builds the software to run it as a multiplayer server game.
    SourcesPlanet Minecraft listing
  11. Winter, into 2013MC Zone gets its own forum. It becomes the center of the community: players talk about the games, share maps, apply to be moderators and report problems in dedicated sections for team killers, hackers, chat abuse and even unruly staff. The volunteer staff keep it in order the same way they police the servers, deleting spam and offensive posts, stepping in on aggressive language, locking threads and banning repeat offenders, all under a published set of forum rules.SourcesSite without a forum (Nov 2012)Forum announcementProject Ares (2012)ForumForum rules

  12. 2013

  13. FebruaryMC Zone passes 160,000 registered users. So many players pile into Hunger Games, Sky Wars and Volmaria, its factions server, that it moves to a bigger machine with two Xeon E5-2620 processors and 64 GB of RAM. By now the servers are connected: joining Hunger Games, Sky Wars or Volmaria drops players into a central hub with signs for every game, and a contest is held to build a better one. As the network grows it ends up running 20 or more servers for each of its busiest games.SourcesAnnouncementHub contest
  14. February to MarchTwo new games arrive. Nexus, a team deathmatch game, opens in public beta on February 26, and Survival Games launches on March 13. Survival Games is based on the map series Vareide began in April 2012, and unlike Hunger Games it's played on hand-built maps, with around 24 players and no kits: everyone loots chests instead. MC Zone also gets its servers onto Minecraft 1.5 before the server software officially supports it.SourcesMC Zone on TwitterNexus betaSurvival GamesMinecraft 1.5
  15. March 23MC Zone hosts the Sky Wars 2.0 Tournament with three teams of four: one led by JJtCool, one by GameplayerHD and one made up of MC Zone staff, with ExplodingTNT among the players.SourcesAnnouncementVideo (JJtCool)
  16. April 19Nebula joins MC Zone. Originally developed by Haribo98 (also known as ParaPenguin), it pits two to four teams against each other in modes like team deathmatch and capture the monument, modeled on the games of Project Ares (later Overcast Network), one of the most popular servers at the time. Haribo98 joins the staff to keep building it. A new lobby and an in-game credits system follow soon after.
    SourcesAnnouncementVideo (JJtCool)
  17. SpringThe website is redesigned as Minecraft Zone, and each game's domain now leads to its page on the site: mc-hg.org, the original Hunger Games address, along with skywars.co, nxmc.org and volmaria.com.SourcesAnnouncementv3Source on GitHubDomain redirects
  18. May 2MC Zone's live player counter reads 2,229 online, the highest figure in any archived capture.SourcesArchived page
  19. Mayavicus.net appears for the first time, as Avicus RPG: a planned role-playing game in Minecraft, though it isn't decided yet whether it will be part of MC Zone. The idea is something like RuneScape in first person, with skills and levels, NPCs, mobs, pets and quests.
    SourcesArchived page
  20. May to JuneMC Zone's game servers are rebuilt on a shared plugin framework, with 114 commits in two months, 89 of them in May. Ghost Squadron, a minigame SethBling released that May with maps by Vechs, arrives as a server game in which every player is invisible to the other team. Pets arrive too: baby animals that follow their owner around, can be renamed and, for sheep, recolored.
    SourcesSource on GitHubVideo (SethBling)Video (Mrules 2001)
  21. JuneMC Zone approaches 500,000 users. funkystudios rewrites the website from PHP into Ruby on Rails and relaunches it with forums, player profiles and match statistics.SourcesSummer newsLaunch postv4
  22. JulyMC Zone moves to Minecraft 1.6 on July 1, opens a new shop at shop.mczone.co and opens registration for a Nebula tournament.Sources1.6 updateNew shopRegistration
  23. SummerIn the game's server list one summer day, MC Zone shows 965 players online out of 3,600 slots.
    SourcesMinecraftServers.org banner (2013)
  24. SummerA creative community grows around the games where the map is the game. funkystudios takes the idea from Halo's Forge mode, where players build their own maps for everyone to play, and brings it to Minecraft at scale. In The Walls, Survival Games, Nexus and Nebula, how a map looks and feels, where the chests sit and where the ores are buried all shape how a match plays out. A whole group of map builders forms around them, each developing their own style and approach. They share their maps on the forum, other players give feedback and vote for their favorites, and the best go into rotation with their builders credited in game and on the website. Builders post their world downloads, and funkystudios sets each chosen map up with a short config file listing its title, teams, spawn points and creators. Here is the Nexus template:

    color: red
    spawn: 10,10,10,90,0

    spawn: 0,0,0,270,0

  25. August to OctoberTo celebrate 500,000 registered users, MC Zone plans "Battle for Victory", a Nebula tournament across all of its game modes, with teams of eight plus four substitutes and a first date of August 16. Nebula is busy: the morning before, about 20 public matches of 44 to 117 players start within 40 minutes. Teams coordinate over Skype, since MC Zone has no voice server of its own, and players start asking for a TeamSpeak server. Twenty-four teams qualify, but the tournament is postponed several times as the staff take over running it.SourcesNebula pageTournament detailsTournament update (Skype)TeamSpeak request
  26. Late AugustMC Zone passes roughly 1 million recorded games, an estimated half of all games played, since tracking started after some games launched. Survival Games leads with 253,000, followed by Sky Wars with 205,000 and The Walls with 165,000. Every match gets its own stats page, showing who played and how they did, and this turns out to be key to MC Zone's success. With monthly and all-time Top 100 leaderboards for every game, players compete to climb them, study their own kill/death ratios and show off on the forums, where one thread collects seven pages of players' best Nebula kill streaks, topped by G_SKIPPY's 219. The all-time leaders that month are izjust4fun in Hunger Games (523 wins, 6,698 kills), vGuap in The Walls (408 wins at a 15.98 kill/death ratio) and Sezsor in Sky Wars (507 wins).SourcesHunger Games leaderboardWalls leaderboardKill streak threadSky Wars matchHunger Games match
  27. SeptemberStaff announce that MC Zone is being rebuilt under a new name: Avicus. The goal is less lag and fewer bugs, better maps, a new website and a tighter lineup of games, so unpopular modes like Ghost Squadron are likely to go, Nebula and The Walls stay, and new minigames are on the way. A new lobby is commissioned for Avicus from HyperScale, with areas for Nebula, The Walls and Control the Hill, a new game planned for the launch.
    SourcesAnnouncement threadAvicus on Twitter (Oct 2013)Video (FightToFight, player survey)
  28. NovemberMC Zone becomes the Avicus Network, the same community rebuilt as its next version.Sourcefunkystudios' recollection
  29. December 23Players in Santa outfits gather for a Christmas party in the Avicus lobby, whose game areas now include Avicade, Elimination and Nebula.

Perspectives

Looking back

Keenan (funkystudios), October 2026

I was 14 when I started TerraScape on a computer at home, mostly so my friends and I would have somewhere to play, and I really didn't know what I was doing. One of the oldest photos I have from that time shows me at my desk typing "it doesnt seem like the shop is working lol" into chat, which sums up those first months pretty well.

That desk is also where I learned to program. I couldn't write a plugin from scratch yet, so I decompiled other people's and changed them until they did what I wanted, usually with players online waiting for me to fix whatever I had just broken. A lot of what I was chasing came from other games, and the biggest idea came from Halo, where my favorite part had always been Forge, with players building their own maps and sharing them for everyone to play. I wanted that in Minecraft, and in the end The Walls, Survival Games and Nexus all ran on maps built by players, with the best of them going into rotation for thousands of people.

None of that would have happened without the people around it. At first it was a few friends from middle school who built with me and chipped in to keep the server running, and before long people I had never met were showing up to build maps, run tournaments, moderate for free and argue on the forums. my0445316 was one of the earliest, and one of TerraScape's first financial supporters, paying for the server when it needed paying for and bringing TerraScape back after I had shut it down. Having someone believe in the idea that much, that early, is a big part of why I kept going.

He was far from the only one, and I still remember every username in the People section above, because each of them is part of this story. Between them, they're the reason I became a software engineer, since keeping MC Zone running is how I learned to code and they gave me a reason to keep learning. I hope it gave them what it gave me: a sense of belonging, and a place to hang out with friends we only ever knew online.

What I'd do differently

The part I think about most is money. The servers ran on ranks, microtransactions and later subscriptions, and a lot of that money came from kids. Parents sometimes emailed asking for refunds, and when we shut a game down, the perks people had paid for went with it, which I'd handle very differently now. I'd also be kinder, because reading old emails I can see I was often hard on people who were helping for free, Haribo98 among them, and they deserved more patience and a lot more thanks.

I'd give more credit, too. Players built many of the maps our games ran on simply because they loved building, and I wish we had recognized them better. The same goes for other servers, especially Project Ares (later Overcast Network), which we took a lot of ideas from, sometimes more than we should have.

What I try to remember is that everyone running MC Zone was a kid. The admins, moderators and developers, me included, did all of this after school for tens of thousands of players every week and more than a million over the years, which is more responsibility than most kids that age ever have, and more than any of us understood at the time.

Mostly, though, I'm grateful to everyone who played, built, moderated, supported the servers financially or just hung out. If you were there, I'd love to hear from you.

Source code

  • thekeenant/mczone-web: The PHP website from spring 2013 (v3): about 90 files including the forum, shop, profiles and game pages.
  • thekeenant/mczone: The game server plugins from mid-2013: a core library plus The Walls, Survival Games, Nexus, Ghost Squadron, lobbies, parkour and pets, about 16,000 lines of Java.
  • Avicus/avicore-maps: The Avicus map repository, May 2014 to September 2016: 275 maps for Nebula, Avicade, Nexus, The Walls, Survival Games and Control the Hill, each with its own map.yml, from 78 contributors across more than 7,000 commits.
